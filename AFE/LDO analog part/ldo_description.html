1.8 V LDO regulator — afe_ldo_tr (3.3 V → 1.8 V, xh018)

Transistor-level low-dropout regulator for the analog 1.8 V domain VDD18A (PLL, CDR, crystal oscillator) of the USB 2.0 ULPI PHY. Netlist: [afe_ldo_tr.sp](afe_ldo_tr.sp); datasheet: [datasheet/ldo_datasheet.md](datasheet/ldo_datasheet.md) (Word: datasheet/ldo_datasheet.docx). Same pins and function as the behavioural model [AFE/veriloga/afe_ldo.va](../veriloga/afe_ldo.va). All values below are ngspice simulations with the X-FAB xh018 models (corner tm, 27 °C, VIN = 3.3 V, COUT = 1 µF) unless stated otherwise.

1. Function and context

The PHY's analog blocks run from 1.8 V, the chip is supplied with 3.3 V (VDD33, 2.97–3.63 V). afe_ldo_tr makes the 1.8 V from VDD33 and supervises it:

  • regulated VOUT = 1.80 V (bandgap reference × 1.517), 0–20 mA (current limit ≈ 75 mA);
  • UVLO: the regulator runs only when VIN > 2.65 V (rising) and stays on down to 2.50 V (falling);
  • LDO_EN: enable input with an absolute threshold, works with 1.8 V or 3.3 V logic;
  • soft start from the RC-filtered reference, active output discharge (≈ 0.6 kΩ) while off;
  • PWR_GOOD 10 µs after VOUT > 1.62 V, cleared at once below 1.55 V; POR_N 100 µs after PWR_GOOD (power-on reset of the digital core).

Expected load: PLL ≈ 1.5 mA, CDR ≈ 1.3 mA, crystal oscillator 0.4–0.8 mA, about 3.5 mA in total; 20 mA is the rated maximum.

3. Why this topology

  • PMOS pass device (common source): the only way to get a low dropout from 3.3 V to 1.8 V with 3.3 V devices and no charge pump. W/L = 2400 µm / 0.5 µm gives 20 mA at a V_SD of ≈ 0.25 V with the gate ≈ 1.1 V below VIN.
  • External 1 µF output capacitor, output pole dominant: the PLL / CDR draw fast current pulses; a µF at the pin supplies them directly, and the loop only has to restore the charge. The loop is then stable with any ceramic capacitor from 0.47 µF up and needs no ESR zero.
  • Source-follower gate buffer: the 2400 µm gate (≈ 3 pF plus Miller) would put a low-frequency pole at the error-amplifier output; the 20 µA PMOS follower moves it above 1 MHz.
  • Bandgap with diodes: xh018 has no bipolar devices in this kit; the p+/n-well diode dp3 (cathode on ground) gives the V_BE. An op-amp forces the two diode branches to the same voltage (no cascodes needed with the 3.3 V headroom), and the same PTAT current (5 µA) biases everything else.
  • Supervisor in the VIN domain, outputs in the VOUT domain: the comparators and delays run from VIN (they must work before VOUT exists); PWR_GOOD and POR_N come from inverters supplied by VOUT, so they are valid 1.8 V logic levels and low whenever VOUT is missing.

4. Circuit

4.1 Bandgap and bias

  • D1 (1×, 100 µm²) on node a; R1 = 10.8 kΩ and D2 (8×) on node b. The op-amp MA1 / MA2 (PMOS input, 20/2) with mirror MN1 / MN2 drives MN3, which sets the current of the diode-connected MP0 (gate node pg); MP1–MP3 copy it. With a = b the branch current is the PTAT current I = Vt·ln 8 / R1 = 4.97 µA.
  • The output branch MP3 → R2 = 95.5 kΩ → D3 (1×) gives VBG = V_BE + (R2 / R1)·Vt·ln 8 = 0.712 V + 0.475 V = 1.187 V. R2 / R1 was swept for the flattest VBG from −40 to 125 °C: 1.182–1.187 V (25 ppm/K box).
  • CCB (2 pF) on the op-amp output compensates the bandgap loop.
  • Start-up: the bandgap has a second, zero-current state. MS3 (long PMOS, ≈ 2 µA) pulls sg to VIN while MS2 (gate VBG) is off; MS1 then pulls pg down and feeds current into node a, so the whole mirror runs. When VBG exceeds ≈ 0.86 V, MS2 grounds sg and MS1 turns off. (A first version with a diode-referenced sg stopped at a = 0.35 V at −40 °C: the start-up NMOS went subthreshold before the op-amp had any bias.)
  • pg biases every current source (5 µA unit, 8/4); MPN + MNB make the NMOS bias nb for the error-amplifier tail.
  • LDO_EN = 0 forces pg to VIN, o1 and sg to ground (bandgap off).

4.2 Reference and soft start

VBG → RSS (5 MΩ) → vr with CSS (10 pF): τ = 50 µs. The RC sets the soft start and filters the bandgap noise above 3.2 kHz. MSS discharges CSS while the regulator is off, so every enable starts from 0.

Because the error amplifier has NMOS inputs, it needs ≈ 0.9 V of common mode: VOUT stays at 0 until vr passes that level (≈ 40 µs after enable), rises at the current limit (≈ 70 mA into 1 µF) to ≈ 1.2 V in ≈ 15 µs, and then follows the RC reference. The result is monotonic, without overshoot; 10–90 % takes 75 µs, 90 % is reached 120 µs after enable.

4.3 Error amplifier, buffer, pass device

  • Error amplifier: NMOS pair ME1 / ME2 (20/1) with tail ME0 (10 µA), PMOS mirror ME3 / ME4 (8/2). ME1 takes the feedback fb, ME2 the reference vr; the output eo drives the buffer.
  • Compensation: REA = 2 MΩ in series with CEA = 50 pF from eo to the mirror diode node ed. At DC the amplifier keeps its full gain (≈ 45 dB; regulation error < 0.2 %); above the zero 1 / (2π·REA·CEA) = 1.6 kHz REA lowers the mid-band gain, which keeps the crossover low enough at light load, where the output pole 1 / (2π·R_load·COUT) is lowest. The choice came from a sweep of REA / CEA over 0–50 mA (section 5).
  • Buffer: PMOS source follower MB1 (20/1) with a 20 µA source MB0 (32/4); gp = eo + V_SG. MGD pulls gp to VIN while off.
  • Pass device: MPASS, pe3i 20 µm / 0.5 µm × 120.
  • Feedback divider: RDA 42.20 kΩ, RDD 5.96 kΩ, RDB 13.18 kΩ, RDC 118.66 kΩ (180 kΩ, 10 µA): fb = 0.6592·VOUT, so VOUT = VBG × 1.517 = 1.800 V. The taps pr (×0.7325) and pf (×0.7656) feed the power-good comparator.
  • vinj (0 V source between fb and the ME1 gate) is the loop-gain injection point for the simulation; it has no function in silicon.

4.4 Current limit

MSEN (2/0.5, 1 : 1200 of the pass device, same gate and source) drives its current into RCS = 12 kΩ. At about 75 mA load the sense voltage reaches the threshold of MCL (≈ 0.75 V); MCL's current, mirrored 1 : 2 by MCL1 / MCL2, pulls eo up and so gp up, which limits the pass device. Measured: VOUT −100 mV at 75 mA, 84 mA into a 1 Ω short; the output recovers in 23 µs without overshoot when the short is removed.

4.5 Enable, UVLO, power good

  • LDO_EN: NMOS MI (4/1) against a 1/40 PMOS load (≈ 2 µA): threshold 0.76 V at 27 °C (0.83 V at −40 °C, 0.66 V at 125 °C), independent of the logic level driving it; two inverters give en_in / en_inb.
  • bg_ok: MG (2/2, gate VBG) against a long PMOS: high once VBG > ≈ 0.85 V. The UVLO comparator output is only trusted with bg_ok (the comparators are unbiased while the bandgap is off). While disabled, MGO pulls bgb to VIN (bg_ok = 0).
  • UVLO: VIN divider RUA 788 kΩ / RUB 52.7 kΩ / RUC 659.3 kΩ (1.5 MΩ, switched off by MUS while disabled). A tap switch selects utr (×0.4395, VIN = 2.70 V at VBG) while the regulator is off and utf (×0.4746, 2.50 V) while on, which gives the hysteresis. Simulated: on at 2.645 V, off at 2.497 V.
  • on = en_in · uv_ok enables the soft start, the buffer and the discharge.
  • Power good: the same tap scheme on the VOUT divider: pr (1.62 V) while PWR_GOOD is low, pf (1.55 V) while high, compared with VBG; gated with on.
  • Comparators (ldo_comp): PMOS-input OTA (5 µA), NMOS common-source second stage with a 1.25 µA load and an inverter. The second stage swings rail to rail, so the logic after it draws no shoot-through current. The enable pins en / enb pull the internal nodes to defined levels while disabled (co low, o2 high, out low).
  • Delays: a ≈ 0.3 µA source (MPD1, 1/8) charges CPGD = 1.6 pF to the trip point of a Schmitt trigger: 10.4 µs to PWR_GOOD. The second stage (CPRD = 16 pF) gives 106 µs to POR_N. Both capacitors are discharged at once when the input falls, so PWR_GOOD falls 25 ns after VOUT drops below 1.55 V or the regulator is disabled.
  • Discharge: MDIS (6/1, ≈ 0.6 kΩ at tm, ≈ 1 kΩ at ws 125 °C) pulls VOUT to ground while off: VOUT < 10 % 1.4 ms after LDO_EN = 0 with 1 µF.
  • No floating node while disabled: besides MGO and the comparator enables, MSO grounds the start-up gate sg and MEO pulls eo to VIN (MB1 off). A first version without them drew up to 170 µA of shoot-through current in the off state (bgb floating at mid-supply at ws, −40 °C); now the off current is ≈ 1 nA.

6. Board / integration

  • COUT: X7R / X5R ceramic, 1 µF (0.47–2.2 µF effective, including DC-bias derating), ESR ≤ 0.5 Ω, placed at the VDD18A pin. 10 µF is stable only with a load of at least 1 mA (section 5) and slows the soft start (current limit).
  • The loads (PLL, CDR, XOSC) should have their own local decoupling; the LDO does not need a minimum load (the divider is its load).
  • LDO_EN can be tied to VIN for an always-on supply; the UVLO and POR_N then sequence the power-up.
  • Resistors: high-resistance poly (rhr, ≈ 1 kΩ/□) for RSS, REA, the dividers and the bandgap; R1 / R2 and the divider sections should be built from one unit resistor for ratio matching. Capacitors: MIM (or MOS for CEA, CSS). The netlist uses ideal R / C (the PDK resistor models were not converted).

9. Open points

  • Noise: 237 µV rms, mostly below 1 kHz: flicker noise of the bandgap op-amp's NMOS mirror MN1 / MN2 (4/4), amplified by ≈ 10 in the bandgap and only filtered above 3.2 kHz. Larger devices (MN1 / MN2 8/32, MA1 / MA2 40/4) give 88 µV, but slow the bandgap loop (VBG settles in ≫ 300 µs at −40 °C and rings on line steps): this needs a re-compensated bandgap op-amp. The PLL tracks supply noise below its loop bandwidth, so low-frequency noise matters less there than for the CDR.
  • Soft start: the NMOS-input error amplifier gives the current-limited first part of the start-up (section 4.2). A PMOS-input pair (or a level-shifted feedback) would make the whole start a linear 50 µs ramp.
  • Resistors / capacitors are ideal: the poly resistor TC and voltage coefficient, the MIM / MOS capacitor models, and the layout parasitics (pass-device gate, VOUT routing) still need to be included.
  • Accuracy / trim: untrimmed −3.8 / +3.1 % over the 3 σ process corners. A 4-bit trim of R2 or of the divider (≈ 0.6 % steps, AFE trim registers 30h–3Fh) would give about ±1 %.
  • Mismatch: no Monte Carlo (the dp3 model is a hand-written B-source). VBG offset from MA1 / MA2 mismatch is amplified by ≈ 10, another reason for the trim.
  • COUT range: 2.2 µF at no load has 43° phase margin, 10 µF needs ≥ 1 mA of load (section 5); a larger divider current would widen the range.
  • The dp3 model covers only the ideal diode current; high-temperature leakage of the junctions is not modelled.
